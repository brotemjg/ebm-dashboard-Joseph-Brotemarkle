# Organizational Evidence: Methods & Acquisition

## Goal

I wanted to use professional data sources to understand:

1. How common is remote/hybrid work right now? (Is everyone else doing this?)
2. Does the job market favor companies that offer hybrid, or does it not really matter?

## Data sources I used

### Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)
- Their telework and time-use tables
- Current Population Survey (CPS) data showing telework by job type and industry
- American Time Use Survey (ATUS) data on where people work
- BLS research reports on remote work and productivity
- www.bls.gov

The BLS is the government agency that tracks employment data, so their numbers are reliable and comprehensive.

### U.S. Census / USAFacts
- Data on how many workers usually work from home
- Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS)
- USAFacts compilations of work-from-home trends
- Census.gov, USAFacts.org

The Census gives us national-level data on workforce trends.

### Work-from-Home Research (Barrero et al.)
- An ongoing research project tracking WFH in the U.S. economy
- They run the Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes (SWAA)
- Their findings are published in peer-reviewed journals and updated regularly
- WFHresearch.com

This is academic research specifically focused on understanding the work-from-home phenomenon.

## My logic model

Here's how I think this change will work:

**X (The Change): Move from fully online to hybrid (2-3 days in office/week)**

This is what I'm proposing - bring people into the office 2-3 days per week instead of staying fully remote.

**M1 (What happens first): Reduced isolation & better collaboration**

By bringing people together in person, I expect them to feel less isolated and be able to collaborate better through face-to-face interaction.

**M2 (What happens next): Higher job satisfaction / thriving**

The combination of less isolation plus keeping some flexibility should improve how people feel about their job and their overall wellbeing.

**Y (The Ultimate Goal): Lower turnover and maintained performance**

Fewer people quit (which saves money on hiring and training), while we keep performance at the same level or better.

This logic model helps me figure out what evidence I need: data showing hybrid is now standard (context), proof that hybrid reduces isolation (M1), proof that it improves satisfaction (M2), and proof that this all leads to better retention (Y).
