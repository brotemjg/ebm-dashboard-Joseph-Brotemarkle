# Assessment: Monitoring & Evaluation Plan
# How I'll know if hybrid work actually improves retention and wellbeing

## 1. Logic Model (X → M → Y)

This shows the logical chain from what I'm doing to why it should work to what results I expect.

### X - Intervention (What I'm implementing)

**Implement a structured hybrid work policy for remote-capable employees:**
- 2 anchor days in office per week
- 3 days remote  
- Supported by manager training and clear team norms

### M - Mechanisms (How/why it should work)

**Improved social connection and collaboration**
- More face-to-face time strengthens relationships and informal knowledge sharing

**Reduced isolation and better wellbeing**
- Employees spend some time in the office but retain flexibility on other days
- Less lonely than full remote, but more flexibility than full office

**Clearer norms and fairness**
- Consistent expectations reduce uncertainty and perceived unfairness between teams

### Y - Outcomes (What results I expect)

**Primary outcome:**
- Lower voluntary turnover rate among hybrid-eligible employees

**Secondary outcomes:**
- Maintained or improved individual performance ratings
- Improved self-reported job satisfaction and wellbeing
- Improved sense of belonging and team cohesion

### The complete logic model

**Structured hybrid policy (X) → more connection, lower isolation, clearer norms (M) → lower quits + stable/improved performance and wellbeing (Y)**

## 2. Evaluation Design

I'll use a **quasi-experimental before/after design** with internal comparisons. This isn't as strong as a randomized trial, but it's realistic for my organization and still gives me useful data.

### Primary comparison

**Compare hybrid-eligible employees' outcomes in the 12 months BEFORE implementation vs 12 months AFTER implementation**

- This tells me if things got better, worse, or stayed the same after we made the change

### Secondary comparison (if possible)

**Compare hybrid-eligible employees to control groups:**

**Option A:** Employees who remain fully remote (e.g., people who live out of state and weren't required to come in)

**Option B:** Employees with no change in work pattern (e.g., on-site roles that were already in-person full-time)

This helps me rule out alternative explanations - if the hybrid group improves but the comparison groups don't, I can be more confident the hybrid policy caused the improvement.

## 3. What I'm Measuring - Outcomes & Metrics

### Primary Outcome: Voluntary Turnover

**Metric:** Voluntary turnover rate for hybrid-eligible employees
- Formula: Number of voluntary quits ÷ average headcount
- **Data source:** HRIS / payroll system
- **Time points:** 
  - Baseline: 12 months pre-implementation
  - Follow-up: 6 months and 12 months post-implementation
- **Target:** Reduce voluntary turnover by at least 20% compared to baseline

### Secondary Outcome 1: Performance

**Metric:** Distribution of performance ratings (or objective performance metrics if available)
- **Data source:** Annual performance review system
- **Time points:** Compare pre-implementation year to post-implementation year
- **Target:** No decline in average performance; ideally slight improvement in team-based work
- **Why this matters:** Need to make sure hybrid doesn't hurt productivity

### Secondary Outcome 2: Wellbeing and Job Satisfaction

**Metrics:** Survey scores - specific questions below

**Data source:** Short pulse survey (8-10 questions total, 5-7 minutes to complete)
- **Time points:** Baseline (2-3 weeks before pilot), Month 6, Month 9, Month 12
- **Target:** Improvement of at least 10% on wellbeing and satisfaction mean scores
- **Scale:** 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) for most items

**Specific survey questions:**

1. **Job satisfaction:** "Overall, I am satisfied with my job."
   - Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree)
   - Expected baseline: ~3.5-3.7; Target 12-month: ~3.9-4.1

2. **Burnout (reverse scored):** "I often feel burned out or emotionally exhausted from my work."
   - Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree)
   - Lower is better; Expected baseline: ~3.0-3.2; Target 12-month: ~2.5-2.8

3. **Overall wellbeing:** "I would rate my overall wellbeing (physical, mental, emotional) as:"
   - Scale: 1 (Very Poor) to 5 (Excellent)
   - Expected baseline: ~3.4-3.6; Target 12-month: ~3.7-4.0

4. **Work-life balance:** "I have a good balance between my work responsibilities and my personal life."
   - Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree)
   - Expected baseline: ~3.6-3.8; Target 12-month: ~3.9-4.2

5. **Stress:** "How often do you feel stressed because of work?"
   - Scale: 1 (Almost Never) to 5 (Almost Always)

**Metrics:** Survey items below

**Data source:** Same pulse survey
- **Time points:** Baseline, 6 months, 12 months
- **Target:** Improvement compared to fully remote baseline - expecting 0.3-0.5 point increase on 5-point scale (isolation should decrease with hybrid)

**Specific survey questions:**

6. **Team connection:** "I feel connected to my team members."
   - Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree)
   - Expected baseline (fully remote): ~3.2-3.4; Target 12-month (hybrid): ~3.6-3.9

7. **Organizational belonging:** "I feel like I belong at this organization."
   - Scale: 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree)

8. **Team collaboration quality:** "My team collaborates effectively to get work done."

## 5. Alternative Explanations & How I'll Address Them

When I see changes in turnover or performance, I need to make sure it's actually because of the hybrid policy and not something else:

### Alternative Explanation 1: Macro labor market changes

**Example:** Maybe the whole economy went into recession and everyone's turnover dropped

**How I'll address it:** Compare my turnover with industry benchmarks or overall company patterns, not just the hybrid group in isolation

### Alternative Explanation 2: Compensation or benefit changes

**Example:** Maybe we gave everyone a big raise at the same time we launched hybrid

**How I'll address it:** Document any major HR policy changes and note them in my interpretation. If big changes coincide with hybrid launch, I'll interpret results cautiously

### Alternative Explanation 3: Organizational restructuring

**Example:** New leadership, mergers, layoffs

**How I'll address it:** Note the timing of any major organizational changes. If they happen during the hybrid pilot, acknowledge this limits what I can conclude

### Alternative Explanation 4: Measurement changes

**Example:** Maybe we changed our performance rating system at the same time

**How I'll address it:** Where possible, use consistent metrics before and after. If metrics change, avoid over-interpreting differences

## 6. Sample Size & Statistical Power

### For Turnover Analysis (Primary Outcome)

**Goal:** Detect a 20% reduction in voluntary turnover rate with 80% statistical power

**Assumptions:**
- Baseline voluntary turnover rate: 15% annually (15 quits per 100 employees)
- Target turnover rate after hybrid: 12% annually (3 fewer quits per 100 employees)
- This is a 20% relative reduction: (15-12)/15 = 20%
- Significance level: α = 0.05 (standard)
- Desired power: 80% (standard for applied research)

**Sample size needed:**
Using proportions test for 15% vs 12% with power 0.80:
- Need approximately **1,500-2,000 employee-years of observation**
- Translation:
  - If 200 hybrid-eligible employees: 12 months pre + 12 months post = 400 employee-years each period (800 total) **Underpowered**
  - If 300 hybrid-eligible employees: 12 months pre + 12 months post = 600 employee-years each period (1,200 total) **Marginally powered**
  - If 500 hybrid-eligible employees: 12 months pre + 12 months post = 1,000 employee-years each period (2,000 total) **Adequately powered**

**What this means:**
- With 200-300 employees, I may not have enough statistical power to detect a 20% reduction, especially if effect is smaller than expected
- **Alternative approach if sample is small:** Use longer time periods (18-24 months) or accept lower power and interpret results cautiously
- Even without statistical significance, a reduction in absolute number of quits is still meaningful for the business

**Minimum detectable effect:**
- With 200 employees (12 months pre/post): Can reliably detect ~30-40% reduction in turnover
- With 500 employees (12 months pre/post): Can reliably detect ~15-20% reduction in turnover

### For Survey Analysis (Secondary Outcomes)

**Goal:** Detect a 0.3-point improvement on 5-point Likert scale (small to medium effect size, ~0.3-0.4 SD)

**Assumptions:**
- Baseline mean: 3.5, Standard deviation: 1.0 (typical for Likert scales)
- Target mean: 3.8 (0.3-point improvement)
- Cohen's d effect size: 0.30 (small-medium)
- Desired power: 80%, α = 0.05

**Sample size needed:**
Using paired t-test (before/after within same employees):
- Need approximately **90-120 survey respondents** for 80% power to detect 0.3-point change

**What this means:**
- If I have 200 eligible employees and get 70% response rate: 140 respondents **Adequately powered**
- If I have 150 eligible employees and get 60% response rate: 90 respondents **Minimally powered**
- **Target response rate: 70%+ to ensure adequate power**

**Strategies to maximize response rate:**
- Keep survey short (5-7 minutes max)
- Send reminders (3 total: initial + 2 reminders over 2 weeks)
- Leader endorsement (email from CEO or department head encouraging participation)
- Small incentive (raffle for $50-100 gift cards, 5 winners)
- Communicate how results will be used
- Anonymous to encourage honesty

## 7. Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis: Voluntary Turnover

**Hypothesis:** Voluntary turnover rate will be significantly lower in the 12 months after hybrid implementation compared to the 12 months before.

**Analysis method 1: Chi-square test of proportions**
- Compare: (# of quits in 12 months post) / (average headcount post) vs (# of quits in 12 months pre) / (average headcount pre)
- Test statistic: Chi-square test for difference in proportions
- Interpretation: p < 0.05 means turnover change is statistically significant

**Analysis method 2: Survival analysis (if I want more detail)**
- Use Cox proportional hazards model or Kaplan-Meier survival curves
- Advantage: Can control for tenure, demographics, job level
- Allows me to see if hybrid affects time-to-quit differently for different groups

**Effect size:** Calculate relative risk reduction
- Example: If baseline turnover = 15% and post-hybrid = 12%
- Relative risk reduction = (15% - 12%) / 15% = 20%
- Absolute risk reduction = 15% - 12% = 3 percentage points

**Practical significance threshold:**
- Even if not statistically significant, a reduction of 3+ quits per 100 employees annually (~20% reduction) is practically meaningful
- Will report both statistical significance (p-value) and practical significance (effect size)

### Secondary Analysis 1: Performance Ratings

**Hypothesis:** Performance ratings will not decline (and may improve slightly) after hybrid implementation.

**Analysis method: Wilcoxon signed-rank test or paired t-test**
- Compare: Average performance rating before vs after hybrid for same employees
- If ratings are ordinal (1-5 scale): Use Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-parametric)
- If many employees and approximately normal distribution: Can use paired t-test
- Interpretation: p < 0.05 means significant change; interested in showing no decline (non-inferiority)

**Effect size:** Cohen's d
- Small effect: d = 0.2
- Medium effect: d = 0.5
- Large effect: d = 0.8
- I expect d close to 0 (no change) or small positive (0.1-0.3 improvement)

### Secondary Analysis 2: Survey Outcomes (Wellbeing, Satisfaction, Belonging)

**Hypothesis:** Survey scores will improve from baseline to 12 months.

**Analysis method: Repeated-measures ANOVA or paired t-tests**
- Compare: Baseline → Month 6 → Month 12 for each survey item
- Use paired t-tests for simple before/after comparisons (baseline vs Month 12)
- Use repeated-measures ANOVA if want to model trajectory over all 3 time points
- Control for multiple comparisons using Bonferroni correction (divide α by number of tests)

**Key survey items to analyze:**
1. Job satisfaction (expect +0.3 to +0.5 points)
2. Wellbeing (expect +0.3 to +0.5 points)
3. Team connection (expect +0.4 to +0.6 points - biggest expected improvement)
4. Isolation - reversed (expect -0.4 to -0.6 points - decreased isolation)

**Subgroup analyses (exploratory):**
- Break down by: job function, commute length, caregiver status, tenure
- Look for moderators: Does hybrid work better for some groups than others?
- Be cautious: Multiple subgroup analyses increase risk of false positives

## 8. Implementation Fidelity - Making Sure It's Actually Implemented

Even the best policy won't work if it's not actually implemented as designed. I need to track whether we're doing what we said we'd do.

### Content fidelity (What must be implemented)

**Essential components:**
- Clearly defined hybrid policy for eligible roles (2 anchor days + 3 remote)
- Manager training on hybrid leadership
- Documented team norms (or "team hybrid agreements")

**How I'll check:** Review policy documentation, training records, team agreement documents

### Dose (How much is needed)

**Minimum exposure:**
- Employees should actually attend the agreed in-office days most weeks (not just ignore the policy)
- Sustained exposure for at least 6-12 months before judging outcomes

**How I'll check:** Badge swipe data or desk bookings showing attendance patterns

### Moderators (What might strengthen or weaken the effects)

These are factors that might make hybrid work better or worse for different people:

**Commute length & cost**
- **What to expect:** Longer/more expensive commutes may reduce the positive impact of hybrid on wellbeing and retention
- **How to track:** Survey item asking about commute time and cost

**Job type**
- **What to expect:** Collaborative, project-based work may benefit more from in-person days than highly independent work
- **How to track:** Compare results by job function or department

**Caregiver status / disability**
- **What to expect:** Employees with caregiving responsibilities or disabilities may experience hybrid differently; they may need additional flexibility or accommodations
- **How to track:** Demographic data and accommodation requests

**Manager quality**
- **What to expect:** Good managers can make hybrid work inclusive and fair; poor managers may generate inequity and dissatisfaction
- **How to track:** Include manager effectiveness questions in pulse surveys; look for patterns by team

## 9. Data Collection Procedures & Quality Control

### Turnover Data Collection

**Data source:** HRIS system (Workday, BambooHR, ADP, or similar)

**Procedure:**
1. HR analytics team pulls monthly separation reports
2. Classify each separation as: Voluntary (employee-initiated) vs Involuntary (termination/layoff) vs Retirement
3. Calculate monthly turnover rate: (# voluntary quits this month) / (average headcount this month) × 100
4. Track 12-month rolling average to smooth out monthly volatility
5. **Quality check:** Compare total separations to payroll changes to ensure no missed records

**Data elements needed:**
- Employee ID (de-identified for analysis)
- Separation date
- Separation type (voluntary/involuntary/retirement)
- Department/team
- Tenure (to control for natural turnover patterns)
- Job level (entry/mid/senior)

**Frequency:** Monthly pull, with quarterly aggregation and analysis

### Survey Data Collection

**Platform:** Qualtrics, Culture Amp, SurveyMonkey, or Google Forms (depending on budget and need for advanced analytics)

**Distribution procedure:**
1. HR sends email invitation with survey link to all hybrid-eligible employees
2. Email includes: purpose, estimated time (5-7 minutes), anonymity assurance, deadline (2 weeks), incentive details
3. Reminder #1 at Day 7 (to non-respondents only if platform allows)
4. Reminder #2 at Day 12 (final reminder)
5. Survey closes at Day 14

**Response rate tracking:**
- Monitor daily response rate
- If below 50% after Week 1, send mid-week boost email from senior leader
- Target: 70%+ response rate

**Data quality checks:**
- Flag surveys completed in <2 minutes (possible straight-lining or random clicking) - review and potentially exclude
- Check for response patterns (e.g., all 5s, all 1s) - may indicate low engagement
- Ensure no IP addresses or identifying information collected (true anonymity)

**Data cleaning:**
- Remove duplicate responses (same person taking survey twice)
- Code open-ended responses: Two independent coders for reliability, resolve disagreements through discussion

### Performance Data Collection

**Data source:** Annual performance review system

**Procedure:**
1. HR pulls performance ratings for all hybrid-eligible employees
2. Compare Year -1 (12 months before hybrid) to Year +1 (12 months after hybrid)
3. If ratings are on different scales year-to-year, standardize (z-scores) before comparing
4. **Challenge:** Performance ratings may be biased or inconsistent across managers - interpret cautiously

**Data elements needed:**
- Employee ID (de-identified)
- Performance rating (numerical or categorical)
- Manager ID (to control for manager effects)
- Department/team

### Office Utilization Data Collection

**Data source:** Badge swipe system or desk booking platform

**Procedure:**
1. IT or facilities team pulls weekly reports showing: Employee ID, Date, Building entry time
2. Calculate: % of hybrid employees who came in on each anchor day
3. Track trends: Is attendance declining over time? Are certain teams/days underutilized?

**Target metric:** 70%+ attendance on anchor days (allows for sick days, PTO, travel)

**Flags for concern:**
- <50% attendance on anchor days → policy not being followed, need to investigate why
- Wide variation between teams → inconsistent implementation, need manager support

## 10. Contingency Plans - What If Things Go Wrong?

### Contingency 1: Very Low Survey Response Rate (<50%)

**Problem:** Can't draw conclusions with low sample

**Mitigation actions:**
- Extend survey window by 1 week
- Offer larger incentive (e.g., 10 × $25 gift cards instead of 5 × $50)
- Have department heads personally encourage their teams
- Conduct targeted phone/video interviews with random sample of non-responders to understand if their views differ

**Decision rule:** If still <50% response, acknowledge limitation in report and interpret survey results cautiously; rely more heavily on turnover data (objective)

### Contingency 2: Turnover INCREASES After Hybrid Launch

**Problem:** Opposite of intended effect - policy is backfiring

**Diagnostic questions:**
- Is turnover concentrated in certain teams? (Manager implementation problem)
- Are exit interviews revealing hybrid policy as a reason for leaving? (Direct evidence of policy failure)
- Is turnover among people with long commutes or caregiving responsibilities? (Insufficient flexibility/support)

**Mitigation actions:**
- Immediate pulse survey to understand employee concerns
- Leadership listening sessions to hear feedback directly
- Consider pausing or adjusting policy: More flexibility, more exceptions, better commute support
- Communicate transparently: "We're seeing concerning trends and here's what we're doing about it"

**Decision rule:** If 6-month turnover is >25% higher than baseline, strongly consider modifying or pausing policy

### Contingency 3: Strong Resistance or Fairness Complaints

**Problem:** Employees perceive policy as unfair or poorly implemented

**Diagnostic questions:**
- Are complaints focused on specific teams/managers? (Inconsistent implementation)
- Are complaints about the policy itself or how it's communicated/enforced? (Change management failure)
- Is there a subset of employees (e.g., parents, people with disabilities) disproportionately affected? (Equity issue)

**Mitigation actions:**
- Audit: Are teams implementing consistently? If not, provide additional manager support
- Expand exceptions process: Make it clearer and more accessible
- Offer accommodations: Transit subsidies, flexible anchor days for those with compelling reasons
- Communicate adjustments transparently

**Decision rule:** If fairness scores on Month 6 survey are <3.0 (below "neutral"), conduct deep-dive investigation and make targeted fixes

### Contingency 4: Major Organizational Change During Pilot

**Problem:** Merger, layoffs, leadership change, or other disruption confounds evaluation

**Mitigation actions:**
- Document the timing and nature of the disruption
- If possible, extend pilot period to get "clean" data after disruption settles
- Be transparent in final report: "Results are difficult to interpret due to [confounding event]"
- Consider comparison groups unaffected by the disruption (if any exist)

**Decision rule:** If major disruption occurs in Months 1-6, consider extending pilot to 18 months; if in Months 7-12, acknowledge limitation but still analyze available data

### Contingency 5: Key Data Not Available or Poor Quality

**Problem:** HRIS data incomplete, survey platform crashes, performance data inconsistent

**Mitigation actions:**
- Build relationships with HR analytics and IT early - ensure data access before launch
- Have backup data sources (e.g., manual exit interview logs if HRIS incomplete)
- Test survey platform thoroughly before launch; have IT support on standby during survey launch
- If performance data unavailable, rely more on turnover and survey data

**Decision rule:** If cannot get clean turnover data, evaluation cannot proceed meaningfully - this is the primary outcome

## Summary: How I'll Know If This Worked

**Short answer:** If voluntary turnover drops, performance stays the same or improves, and employees report better wellbeing and belonging, then hybrid worked.

**Timeline:** I'll have preliminary results at 6 months and conclusive results at 12 months.

**Key metrics to watch:**
1. Voluntary turnover rate (most important)
2. Performance ratings (making sure we don't hurt productivity)
3. Wellbeing and satisfaction scores (intermediate outcomes)
4. Belonging and collaboration scores (mechanisms)

**What could mess up my conclusions:** Economic changes, other HR policy changes, organizational restructuring happening at the same time

**How I'll handle uncertainty:** Use comparison groups where possible, document alternative explanations, be honest about limitations in my final report
